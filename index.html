LawKAN: Group-Law Layers for Composition — When Imposing a Known Law Pays Off

arXiv:2611.12567 · November 2026

Paper Code

A group-law layer: an exact composition law in the node, learned local increments on the edges, and a small zero-initialized correction ψ that learns where real hardware deviates from the law.

  • Tiny models — tens to hundreds of parameters — that match or beat networks tens of times larger.
  • A small learned correction that beats classical estimators on real robot-arm calibration, and localizes where the hardware deviates from the law.
  • A cheap pre-training check (the span criterion) that says whether your features can represent the law at all — so you know where the method wins, ties, or loses before you train.

Research interests

  • Composing learned models with exact laws — group structure, geometric deep learning
  • Calibration of robot arms and inertial sensors
  • Tiny, parameter-efficient models for edge and embedded deployment
