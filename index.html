Research

One question, three levels of structure.

The law in the node

An exact, non-commutative group law sits in the node of a self-contained layer. The edge learns only the local step into the group, so composition is representable exactly when the edge features span the chart’s logarithm.

Learning the deviation

Real hardware bends the law. A small, zero-initialized correction ψ is trained in isolation on the frozen exact edge; its shape localizes a law-side deformation and doubles as a test of whether the law holds.

A measured envelope

We report where the recipe gives parity, where it wins, and where it loses — on synthetic composition, three robot vendors, two calibration protocols, consumer IMUs, and a gyroscope. Negative results are kept.

arXiv:2611.12567 cs.LG · cs.RO November 2026

LawKAN: Group-Law Layers for Composition — When Imposing a Known Law Pays Off

Anna Mishchenko, Nikolai Gavrilin

Composition is a non-commutative product. LawKAN puts the exact group law in the node, learns only the local increment on the edge, and adds a zero-initialized correction for hardware deviations. The paper maps when this pays off: a 54-parameter dense edge on fixed-angle SU(2) composition, an isolated 306-parameter correction reaching 1.12–1.14 mm versus 1.29–1.33 mm for Gauss–Newton on real robot arms at N ≥ 60, parity where the law holds, and clear losses where the imposed law is not the task’s.
